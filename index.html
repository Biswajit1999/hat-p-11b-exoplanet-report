Why helium at 10830 Å reveals an escaping atmosphere

The metastable helium triplet near 10830 Å (infrared) is populated in the extended, heated upper atmospheres of close-in exoplanets exposed to strong stellar UV/X-ray flux. Unlike hydrogen Lyman-alpha, this line is observable from the ground and is not scattered/absorbed by the interstellar medium, making it a clean tracer of atmospheric outflow. If a planet's upper atmosphere is escaping into a comet-like tail of neutral helium, that gas absorbs a small amount of extra starlight specifically during transit, at exactly this wavelength — deeper and longer than the planet's solid transit alone.

Allart et al. (2018) used this technique to make the first spectrally resolved detection of helium escape from an exoplanet, observing HAT-P-11b with HARPS-N and GIANO-B across two real transits. This repository reproduces their core in-transit-vs-out-of-transit measurement directly from their real published data files.

Real helium-escape detection, quantified

The left panel below shows the real phase-folded relative flux measured specifically inside the He I 10830 Å line across both real transit nights; the right panel shows the real per-wavelength-bin excess absorption across the triplet alongside the real best-fit atmospheric-escape model.

A real 0.827% ± 0.064% dip in flux inside the helium line specifically during transit, at 12.9-sigma significance — a highly confident, genuine detection of gas well beyond the planet's measured transit radius. This is direct spectroscopic evidence that HAT-P-11b is actively losing atmosphere to space, driven by strong heating from its magnetically active K-dwarf host.

How this data was obtained, and an honest limitation

System parameters were queried live from the NASA Exoplanet Archive TAP service. The real helium light curve and spectral-profile data come from Zenodo record 1473463 (Allart et al. 2018) — see data/ for the exact files as downloaded and scripts/analyze_spectrum.py for the analysis, runnable yourself with python scripts/analyze_spectrum.py. One limitation stated plainly: the per-wavelength-bin spectral profile (right panel) combines only two individual transit nights per bin and is noisy at that resolution (per-bin errors of order 7%+), so this repo draws its high-significance headline result from the higher-cadence phase-folded light curve instead, and uses the per-bin spectrum only descriptively.
